# Tomodachi: working concepts

What the demo proves today, and the rules we've settled on. Plans and open questions are
[GitHub issues](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues); the research behind them is
mapped in [research/README.md](research/README.md).

## The pitch

See the root level README.md

**The core: Tomo comes to you.** You never have to find the discipline to open a learning app every day. Tomo drops in through the day for a 10–30 second moment, then leaves. What it brings comes from a **content source**. Today the only source is the age track (the README's idea). Other sources, like an Anki deck, are ideas ([#11](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/11), [#12](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/12)).

## What works

| Concept | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Lives in the notch** | Tomo sits small beside the notch, opens into a card, and its eyes follow your cursor. While something would count, small Tomo glows a soft, pulsing amber in a still amber light on the island around it, and the resting island grows a little under the pointer. On the other side of the notch it says あそぼ！ (the pack's invite) while something would count, or, dimmed, when words are back ("2:10 PM"), beside a small ring for its level in its own colour. The notch never counts what's waiting. Built on the [Coucou](https://github.com/Louis-CFM/coucou) notch app (MIT code). |
| **First run** | On the Mac, a new learner's first launch opens a short welcome window before Tomo's first visit, following the reference app in [#83](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/83): an egg in the colours of the learner's Tomo rocks until tapped, cracks, and Tomo pops out (ばあ！); Tomo's first word, asked with a guide ("Try it now") and counted like any answer; "You just understood your first Japanese", with when the word comes back and why; how Tomo comes to you by the notch (and that the Dock icon opens Tomodachi); how often (the visit choices); quiet hours (no visits at night); "Let Tomo find you", which on the Mac is opening Tomodachi at login (on, but shown, so it's the learner's choice); and what 1さい to 3さい bring. Then Tomo pops out of the notch. Closing the window counts as done. Learners who already have a Tomo don't see it; a Mac that joins the learner's iCloud Tomo during it gets "welcome back" (おかえり！), then how often, quiet hours and open at login. The Testing menu's **Show the first run** plays it on a copy of the learner's Tomo: nothing is saved, and a setting changes only if it's changed there. On the iPhone it's full screen, with the same hatch and first word, then the reference's own steps: how Tomo comes to you there (a notification, the Lock Screen), the rhythm, quiet hours, "Let words find you" (the only place the notification prompt appears), how to add a widget, and Tomo's Lock Screen card, yes or not now. A phone that joins the iCloud Tomo gets "welcome back", then those steps. |
| **Own character** | Every Tomo is a blob of its own, drawn and animated by our own code. A seed made when it first appears picks what it keeps for life: its colour, its eyes (bars, dots, big shiny ones, sleepy lids, tiny beads; rarely just one eye), a resting mouth, markings in a second colour (a belly, a cap, spots, stripes, two tones) and, from 2さい, something on its head (cat, bunny or bear ears, horns, an antenna, a sprout). So no two learners' Tomos look alike, and the Mac and iPhone show the same one. Each birthday it evolves: it squeezes down glowing and pops out in a new shape and proportions, a bit bigger, flinging drops of its colour; at 2, 4 and 6さい it also gains an extra (rosy cheeks, freckles, a shine, a little mark). Rare shapes are lucky finds. Tomo is made of jelly: wet and glossy, lit from inside, with a light rim and two highlights; it wobbles after every hop, landing and poke, and the amber call glows from inside it. When the app starts, and when the Mac wakes, Tomo says hello: a drop falls from the notch and gathers into Tomo, sleepy, then awake and beaming (on the iPhone, when the app opens). Widgets, the Lock Screen and the app icon show the yellow Tomodachi mascot, as before (so does About). Starting over hatches a new Tomo. It never sits still: it breathes, blinks, follows your cursor, fidgets between events, and dozes off if ignored. With Reduce Motion on (the Mac's or the iPhone's accessibility settings), its hops, shakes and wiggles become a change of face and a small puff, a level-up or a birthday is a glow and star eyes, Tomo fades in and out instead of dripping from the notch and being pulled back up (its hello is a fade with its light), the call glow holds steady, the resting island doesn't grow, the card's contents swap without blurring and its coloured light comes and goes without rippling, and sparkles are fewer and slower; it still breathes, blinks and watches your cursor, and still changes shape at a birthday. Widgets and the Lock Screen card move regardless. Nothing of Coucou's Mochi character is left. |
| **Levels and ages** | Tomo has levels: small sets of words. When 9 in 10 of a level's words reach "knows it", Tomo levels up (Lv 2, Lv 3…): it crouches, leaps with star eyes in a burst of sparkles, lands with a wobble and settles happy, with a happy sound, and the next set unlocks. Some levels are birthdays, like a Pokémon evolving: 1さい speaks single baby words (ワンワン, まんま), 2さい evolves and uses two-word phrases (ワンワン いた！), 3さい has real short conversations. The header shows "1さい · Lv 2". A full-width experience bar under it moves a little with every answer that counts (each stage one of the level's best 9 words reaches, up to "knows it"). Its last tenth is the goal, set apart: it fills only when the level is done, so the bar never looks full before. It never moves back: a slip sets the word back, not the bar, and that word's part turns pale until it's answered right again. Under Tomo's word the card says what's left: "1 more word to Lv 2 · ready now", or "· next at 8:37". Level and age never go down, and Tomo is never younger than its level's age (a Tomo saved before the pack's ages moved catches up when it loads), so each birthday comes exactly where the pack's age goes up. Japanese goes to 6さい (274 levels), English to 3さい (66 levels); Spanish has small placeholders. |
| **Word stages** | Every word has a stage, following WaniKani's published rules in our own code: just heard (1–4) → knows it (5–6) → good at it (7) → loves it (8) → forever (9, never asked again). A right answer moves a word up one stage when it's due, or early once at least half its wait has passed; Tomo asks again after 4 h, 8 h, 1 day, 2 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month, then 4 months (levels 1–2 start faster). Wrong tries in the same round move it down, twice as far from "knows it" up. Right after the hint, it stays. Answering again sooner is practice: Tomo still reacts, nothing moves (the badge says **Practice**) |
| **Seeing Tomo grow** | The same screens on the Mac and the iPhone. **Tomo**: a live Tomo, its age and level, the bar to the next level and what's left ("1 more word to Lv 2 · ready now", or when it's back while it rests), days together, each age with its levels and what it brings (grown / now / later), today's new words, answers and words that got stronger, and Play with Tomo. **Words**: every level, locked ones too, opening at Tomo's level, with each word's picture, reading, meaning, stage and when it's due. On the Mac they're pages of the Tomodachi window: click Tomo's age in the notch header for Tomo, Tomo → Tomo's words (⌘W) for Words. On the iPhone they're tabs. |
| **Saved progress** | Tomo's level, age and every word's stage are saved on the device, one Tomo per language pair, and synced through the learner's iCloud: the Mac and the iPhone share one Tomo. When both already had one, they merge (the higher level, each word's latest answer); a start over reaches the other device too. Every answer is logged, to tune the rules later. Start over (Settings, or the Tomo menu on the Mac) asks first; it's never one click away in the notch. |
| **Understanding before speaking** | 1–2さい: each word is asked the best way it can be: **pick the picture** (about 290 words have one), **do what Tomo asks** (feed / bed / hug), or **pick the meaning** out of three, which works for every word. 3さい: Tomo asks real questions (なに してるの？), and you **pick what it means** (a new question) or **pick a reply that fits** (later reviews), out of three. Typed and spoken answers are switched off for now; when they're back, romaji is read as kana. |
| **Baby talk + grown-up word** | Tomo says ワンワン, and after you get it the card shows "grown-ups say: いぬ（犬）". |
| **Drop-in visits** | Tomo comes out on its own every so often (20 min by default; 10 min to 2 h, or only when clicked, in Settings), starting at launch, but never in quiet hours (9 PM to 8 AM by default, the same setting as the iPhone's; no bounces then either, and clicking Tomo still plays), for up to 3 answers: words that are due first, then at most one new word (10 new a day by default; 5–30 in Settings). A scheduled visit with nothing due or new is skipped. Tomo drips down from the top of the screen on a goo strand that lets go as it lands, and when the card or the peek closes, it's pulled back up before the island folds, then waits small beside the notch. When the card changes to another (the next word, resting, a level-up), its contents blur across. Ignore it for 10 s (20 s for an answer in your own words, switched off for now), and a thin amber line under the card runs out as it goes; pointing at the card, listening or help hold it. On the Mac a visit can peek first instead (Settings; the card is the default, [#19](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/19)): the island widens into a bar where Tomo calls, lit amber from Tomo, with あそぼ！ over the visit's word. Resting the pointer on it for a moment, or a click, opens the card, the word sliding into place, and only then does Tomo ask and say it; after a close, the pointer has to leave the island before hovering opens anything. Ignored, the peek tucks back in when its line runs out, like the card. Or close it with × (or Esc once you've clicked it; a visit never takes your keyboard), and it tucks back in, bouncing now and then until you check in. Visits wait while you're typing and skip if you're away (no permission needed). Tomo follows the notch when displays change. |
| **Always there** | Between visits, click small Tomo any time for free play with no time limit. Playing by choice counts: it brings due words, then new ones (within the daily limit), then words far enough along to review early. When none of those are left, Tomo rests: happy and moving as always, it says "また あとで あそぼ！", and the card says when it's back ("Back at 10:12") and what's left before the next level. Resting holds: when it tucks back in, when you click small Tomo again and when you come back to the iPhone app, it's still resting, never asking again; when something counts while the card is open, Tomo notices and asks it. Practice is a quieter button on the resting card, like WaniKani's Extra Study, and says it won't move the bar. While practicing, the header says "Practice until 6:31 AM" and the bar dims; practice lasts until something counts or Tomo is opened again, and rotates through the words closest to due. |
| **Child-like feedback** | Right: hop, happy wiggle, sparkles, praise. Wrong: shake, > < eyes and ちがう〜. Not understood: head tilt, a "?" and ん？ わかんない… Poked: a squish that flops its ears or sprout, and いたい！ (three pokes make it dizzy). Rest the pointer on Tomo (Mac) or hold a finger on it (iPhone): hearts and a blush, at most every 6 s |
| **Help when you're stuck** | A prototype. Help opens in a large-text panel that grows out of the notch under Tomo's card. Click a word for its card (meaning in your language, the Mac dictionary, Open in Zenbu). Drag across words → "I don't understand" → an explanation of just that part. **Hint** shows the meaning and example answers (click one to use it). **Explain** gives the key parts and a tip, and can ask Tomo to say it simpler. Tomo doesn't leave while help is open. |
| **Every answer has an obvious result** | **✓ Win +1** (green): understood or right picture; the word moves up a stage. **✓ Practice** (blue): right, but nothing counts right now, so the bar doesn't move. **✗ Miss** (red): tried, Tomo didn't get it, or wrong picture. **– No score** (grey): wrong language, or a help request ("what?", なに？); no credit, no penalty. Help requests repeat the question slowly and open the hint. The card's light tells the moment: green for a Win, blue for Practice, gold for a level-up or a birthday, rippling out from Tomo across the card and the island around it, then fading as the card moves on. A Miss and No score get no light: never red. |
| **Sound effects** | Soft blob sounds, no peeps: a visit starting, Win, Practice (a smaller win), Miss (gentle and low, never a buzzer), No score (a questioning "hm?"), a level-up, a birthday, being poked and getting dizzy, eating, love and a yawn. The island blips when it opens, closes and peeks. All synthesized in code, with no audio files; Tomo's sounds get lower and fuller as it grows from 1さい to 6さい, and every age plays each moment equally loud. They're **bubbly**: water-drop bloops and bubble pops. One switch with the voice. |
| **Voice** | Tomo speaks with macOS's voice for the language at a raised pitch, free and offline. Spoken answers (Apple's on-device recognition) are switched off while talking questions are choices, so the app doesn't ask for the microphone. |
| **AI is optional** | Every age is scripted for now (3さい questions are multiple choice). AI is used for word cards and explanations, and will judge typed answers when they come back; without it, a placeholder keyword matcher stands in until the Zenbu offline dictionary system replaces it. |
| **A real Mac app** | Tomodachi has a Dock icon and a main menu: Tomodachi (About, Settings… ⌘,, Quit), Tomo (Drop in now ⌘D, Tomo's words ⌘W, Start Tomo over…). Clicking the Dock icon, or opening the app again, opens the Tomodachi window: a sidebar with Tomo, Words, Settings and About. Tomo still lives in the notch, and its visits never bring the app to the front or take the keyboard; launched at login, only Tomo in the notch shows. The menu bar icon has the same commands, plus Open Tomodachi… and Play with Tomo. |
| **Settings** | The same on the Mac and the iPhone: new words a day, Tomo's voice and sounds, the language Tomodachi's text is in (English or Japanese), and Start Tomo over… (asks first). The Mac adds how often Tomo visits (or only when clicked), how a visit starts (open the card, or peek first), open at login, and quiet hours for the visits; the iPhone adds its reminders (on or off, the rhythm, quiet hours) and Tomo's Lock Screen card. **About** has the version, the privacy policy, support and the credits (the word data's sources and licenses), on both. The testing tools (another age, skip ahead a day, grow without waiting, all on a copy of Tomo in memory) show only after ⌥-clicking the menu bar icon: a Testing menu and page. |
| **Any language pair** | What you speak and what you learn are separate. Packs: Japanese, English, Spanish; interface in English or Japanese, so a Japanese speaker can learn English. The first release is Japanese only, so "I'm learning" shows only with the testing tools (or for a learner already on another language). Details: [languages.md](languages.md). |
| **Reminders (iPhone)** | Tomo comes to you on the iPhone with a notification when words are ready: a new word, or one that's due. The learner picks the rhythm in the first run (15 min, 30 min, hourly, the default, 2 h, 4 h or daily) and quiet hours (9 PM to 8 AM by default), and can change them in Settings. Rules: only when something counts, never sooner than one rhythm after playing, never in quiet hours (a word due at night waits for the morning; daily is each morning), at the rhythm for two days if ignored, then once a morning, and nothing a week after the first, until the learner is back. Each is Tomo speaking at its age, with a translation and what's waiting ("あそぼ！" / "Let's play! · 3 words waiting"). Tapping one opens the app, which is free play, so Tomo asks right away. |
| **On the iPhone** | On TestFlight. Four tabs: **Play**, the same Tomo big and alive, with the same rounds, results and growth as the notch card (opening the app is free play); **Tomo** and **Words**, as on the Mac; and **Settings**, with About as its last row. What's left to grow is under the bar, and when nothing counts Tomo rests on the screen and says when it's back, with Practice as a button. Tap a word in Tomo's line for its word card (the line's meaning from the pack, the AI's meaning of the word if a key is set, Open in Zenbu, and the iPhone's built-in dictionary). Home Screen widgets (small, medium) show Tomo, its age and level, and a red dot with "3 words waiting" when something counts; Lock Screen widgets show the age, level, bar and what's waiting. On the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island (once the learner says yes, in the first run or Settings), a Live Activity shows Tomo beside two rows: what's waiting ("A new word is ready to learn"), then age, level and the experience bar, with a round Play button; when nothing counts, Tomo sleeps and the card says when the next words come ("New words at 10:12"), turning to "ready" by itself. Play opens one round right on the card: Tomo's word and its choices; a miss marks the choice and Tomo shakes it off, a right answer counts like in the app and shows the reading and meaning before the card invites again. Tapping anywhere else opens the app; the compact Dynamic Island says "あそぼ！". On all of these Tomo moves, a new pose every second, and holds still once iOS stops ticking seconds on the Lock Screen. No mic or Explain yet ([#52](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/52)). |
| **Any AI provider** | One adapter: Anthropic's own API, plus anything that speaks the OpenAI chat format (OpenAI, Gemini, OpenRouter, Groq, Ollama, custom). The first release is offline: only Debug builds have AI, where the **AI** page (key in the Keychain) shows with the testing tools, or a key comes from the environment. Release builds (the App Store, TestFlight, the beta, the owner's copy) make no AI calls. Tested with OpenAI `gpt-5.4-mini` (about 2 s a reply) and a local Ollama. |

## Rules we've settled on

- **Tomo comes to you.** The visits are the product; the material is a content source that can be swapped.
- **No study sessions.** Learning happens in short visits. Free play is always available but never required.
- **Tomo misses you, and lets you know.** The longer you stay away, or the more visits you ignore in a row, the more restless, pouty and then lonely Tomo gets, in its own toddler words. It can guilt-trip you, toddler-style (funny, not mean), visit more often or get louder, and coming back is a reunion (planned). Streaks, misses and missed days are data, and can be shown. The one thing Tomo never does is pile up a count of what you have to do (a badge or alert like "12 words to review"): that feels like homework, so waiting shows through Tomo's mood instead. Quiet hours, the learner's own setting, still hold.
- **Grade whether you were understood, not your pronunciation.** If Tomo doesn't get it, it reacts like a child (ん？), not like a teacher.
- **Grows over days, not minutes.** A word only moves up when it's due (or at least halfway there), so knowing it takes several visits over days. You can't cram; practice in between is welcome but doesn't count.
- **Check offline first, call AI only for leftovers** (planned). Most toddler questions have expected answers that can be checked without AI (yes/no, pick an animal or a food). AI handles the long tail.

## How Tomo grows

The rules are in `TomoProgress.swift`; the levels come from the language pack, built from Wordbank's toddler word data (CDI): each word's age is when half of children understand it (by 19 months: 1さい) or say it (24–35 months: 2さい; later: 3さい), earliest first, in levels of 10. **Japanese, up to 6さい:** 2,712 words in 274 levels. Lv 1–15 are 1さい (バイバイ, ママ, ワンワン, まんま…), Lv 16–60 2さい, Lv 61 is 3さい (talking starts), then Lv 92 4さい, Lv 121 5さい, Lv 180–274 6さい. Past 3, the words come from NINJAL's preschool and picture-book word lists with English meanings from JMdict (ages assigned by how many preschoolers used a word and in how many books it appears; an assumption to tune). **English** (for Japanese speakers; each word has a Japanese meaning): 647 words; Lv 1–26 are 1さい (Daddy, Hi, Mommy, Ball, Dog…), Lv 27–56 2さい, Lv 57 talking, Lv 58–66 later words. Built by `mac-demo/scripts/build-levels.py` ([languages.md](languages.md)). The self-test simulates learners with these rules ([verification.md](verification.md)): one who is always right and plays the moment anything counts reaches Lv 2 in 19 hours, 2さい in 24 days and 3さい in 101; one who is right 85% of the time, with 3 visits and 10 answers of free play a day, in 5.5, 99 and 442 days (always right on that schedule: 3さい in 311; with 30 answers of free play: 299). Pacing is [#38](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/38). Spanish still has small placeholder levels.
