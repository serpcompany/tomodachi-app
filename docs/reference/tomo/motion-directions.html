Recommendation

Build Lit clay, and borrow Jelly’s strand for the visits

Lit clay is effect 2 of #137 item 3, soft 3D lighting, done properly. It is one lighting pass over any path, so it works on every silhouette, every tone and every friend. Its cost at rest is three fixed gradients. Its moments are light cues (stage light, gel colour, a spotlight), so its Reduce Motion version is the same moment without the hops. Its bow is a hello that is ours and plainly Japanese.

Clay’s weight makes the drip from #142 read better, not worse. Keep #142’s strand for arrivals from the notch, and use clay’s two hops for the island-to-card move. Paper cut is the strongest second choice if energy at rest matters most, because it redraws at 12 fps. It is also the only direction that makes the iPhone’s one-pose-a-second frame font look intended. Lantern is the riskiest: a light that glows in the notch all day draws the eye.

Tomo is drawn by a JavaScript port of TomoLook.swift and TomoCharacter.swift (via the Decision Lab), extended for this page with material passes, a shared canvas for the island and the card, and a frame rate per Tomo. The four materials, their travels, hellos, badges and bars are sketches for this decision. None is in the app.

The words, levels and counts in the cards are examples. In the app, Tomo’s words come from the language pack and the interface text from ui.<id>.json.
