### 2026-10-11: The word card shows the Mac's dictionary only in the learner's language
The word card's Mac Dictionary section shows an entry from the first dictionary switched on in Dictionary's settings whose languages are the pair's: ウィズダム英和・和英 (ja-en) for an English speaker, スーパー大辞林 (ja) for a Japanese speaker. With none, the section is left out, never a Japanese-only entry for an English speaker ([#198](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/198)). **Why:** the system's own lookup takes the first dictionary with an entry, and 大辞林 comes before ウィズダム, so English speakers got definitions they can't read. Choosing the dictionary needs two calls Apple doesn't publish (`DCSGetActiveDictionaries`, `DCSDictionaryGetIdentifier`, found at run time); guessing the language from the entry's text was ruled out, because 大辞林 answers some words with English acronyms (あさ → ASA, ペン → PEN). The Mac App Store build drops the two calls or has the Zenbu dictionary by then ([#9](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/9)).

### 2026-10-11: The Lock Screen card says what the app says, on the person's own clock
Settled on [#192](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/192): the widgets, the Lock Screen card and the Dynamic Island say what the app says. While anything counts by the app's rule (early reviews too), Tomo wants to play, in #169's amber and never with a count; resting, they show the app's own "Back at" line, which the app writes into the glance, so nothing on the iPhone formats a time of its own. Every time Tomo shows is on the person's 12- or 24-hour setting, in the learner's language (`tomoClockLocale`): a locale made from the language alone put English on a 12-hour clock for someone who chose 24 hours, while iOS drew the card on the device's clock. The hour between them was two rules, not a time zone: the card counted to a word's due time, the app to halfway through its wait.

### 2026-10-11: release-please versions Tomodachi and the Mac app; the iPhone's version stays the App Store's
release-please (SERP's git-workflow.md) keeps one release PR open on `main`, "release: Tomodachi x.y.z". Merging it tags `v<x.y.z>`, publishes the release with its notes and adds them to [CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md). It starts from v0.2.0, the one version tagged by hand (`be3c820`). One package covers the repo, and the Mac app's version (`CFBundleShortVersionString` in `NotchBuddy/project.yml` and its Info.plist) is that version: until 1.0, a `feat:` takes 0.2.0 to 0.3.0 and fixes alone to 0.2.1. The iPhone's version (1.1, the app's and the widgets', in `ios-demo/Tomodachi/project.yml`) stays manual: the owner raises it when a new App Store version opens in App Store Connect. **Why:** an App Store version only goes up and needs a new number only after one ships, while every upload already gets its own build number (#172). Following the repo would take it from 1.1 down to 0.3.0. A release-please component of its own would count only commits under `ios-demo/`, missing TomoCore, which the iPhone shares with the Mac, and would add a second tag and release PR. Revisit when the iPhone ships updates often. **Merging a release PR** publishes, so it needs the owner's go-ahead. release-please opens it with the built-in `GITHUB_TOKEN`, which starts no workflows, so `main`'s required checks never run on it by themselves: close and reopen it right before merging (a person's reopen runs them), or merge it as an admin.

### 2026-10-11: Tomo dances to Music and Spotify from their own notifications
Small Tomo dances while Music or Spotify plays, from the notifications the players post anyway (`com.apple.Music.playerInfo`, `com.spotify.client.PlaybackStateChanged`), reading only their "Player State": no AppleScript, no Automation prompt, no permission. It rests to dance: visits and quiet hours win. **Why:** #130 asked to check first that a sandboxed build (the Mac App Store's, #108) gets them. A throwaway sandboxed app got named distributed notifications with their userInfo from another app, and Apple documents limits only on a sandboxed *sender*. AppKit holds an inactive app's distributed notifications, and Tomodachi is rarely the active app, so it observes them with `.deliverImmediately`. Music already playing at launch shows at the player's next notification (accepted in #130).

### 2026-10-11: The shortcut to play is a Carbon hot key, and there's none until the learner records one
On the Mac, Settings records a shortcut that opens Tomo to play from any app, as a click does: free play, and it takes the keyboard, so Esc closes it; pressed while the card is open it takes the keyboard, or closes Tomo if it has it. It's a Carbon hot key (`RegisterEventHotKey`), so it needs no Accessibility permission. It refuses a key without ⌘, ⌃ or ⌥, ⌘ or ⌘⇧ with one key (every app's), and macOS's own (Spotlight, switching input sources, emoji, Spaces, the lock screen, Force Quit), and says when another app already holds it. **Why none by default:** a hot key takes its combination from every app on the Mac, and any default (⌃⌥T, ⌥⌘T…) belongs to some app or some learner's muscle memory; Tomo shouldn't take a keystroke the learner didn't give it, which is also why visits never take one (2026-10-07, Esc).

### 2026-10-11: Stats show what you and Tomo did, starting with a postcard every Monday
The owner marked all four directions of [Stats, Take Two](reference/tomo/stats-take-two.html) good (Tomo's diary, the word garden, a height pillar, a weekly postcard) and wants them all, in stages, on one stats layer (`TomoStats`, [stats.md](stats.md)): Tomo's week as a postcard and the Today line first, on a screen of its own (Together). Under the rule below, the layer gives streaks, missed days, misses and ignored visits: the Today line shows the streak, the postcard's back the misses and the week's run of days in a row, kept light (no zeros, Tomo asleep through a quiet week), and ignored visits stay off the screens for now, by choice. Nothing counts what's left to do. Export comes with the stats screen. Stats count this device's logs until they sync. **Why:** a postcard carries Tomo, not a chart, and is made to be shared (#29).

### 2026-10-11: Guilt is allowed; only to-do counts are out
The no-guilt rule of 2026-10-10 ("Tomo misses you; it never keeps score", #129) is gone. Tomo can guilt-trip you, toddler-style. Misses, wrong answers and warnings can be red. Stats and Tomo can show streaks, missed days and miss counts. An ignored Tomo can get more intrusive (visit more often, louder), not only more expressive. The one rule left: never a growing count of things to review or do, on a badge, in the notch or in an alert; waiting shows through Tomo's mood. Quiet hours stay, as the learner's own setting. **Why:** the owner: streaks are data, and guilt trips are funny when Tomo does them; a badge of things to do feels like homework.

### 2026-10-10: Tomo misses you: it sulks when ignored, and never shows a count
Tomo may now show that it misses you. Visits ignored in a row and time since you last played raise its mood: restless (more bounces, watching the pointer), then pouty (puffed cheeks, かまって！), then lonely (turned half away, さみしい). Any answer resets it, and coming back is always a happy reunion. It gets more expressive, never more intrusive: no extra visits, no louder sounds, nothing in quiet hours, and after several days it stops escalating and sleeps until you're back. The notch never shows how many words are waiting, so the red dot of 2026-10-03 goes. A setting will choose how needy Tomo is ([#19](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/19)). Planned from the research on [#110](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/110). **Why:** the owner prefers a little guilt to a growing to-do list ("guilt is better than accumulation of todos"), and "never disappointed in you" made Tomo easy to ignore. Neglect reactions are normal for virtual pets, a sulky toddler is in character, and its sulky words are real toddler Japanese, so they teach. Gentle sounds stay: a Miss is still never a buzzer.

### 2026-10-08: Tomo sounds like a blob, not a bird: bubbly, picked by ear
The chick's peeps and chirps are gone: the owner found they no longer made sense for a blob. Three blob palettes replace them, synthesized in code like before: **bubbly** (water-drop bloops and bubble pops), **squishy** (rubbery boings and a jelly wobble) and **toy kalimba** (soft plucked tones). Each covers every moment, now with its own Practice (a smaller win) and dizzy sounds, and gets lower and fuller from 1さい to 6さい (about a fifth lower, with a lower octave and a longer ring); the island's blips don't change with age. Every sound is set to its moment's loudness (K-weighted, peaks under -3 dBFS), so a palette or an age never plays louder than another. The owner listened to all three next to the old bird set, at 1, 3 and 6さい, and picked **bubbly**; the other two are deleted. **Why:** a blob has no beak. Bubbles are the sound a soft, gooey thing makes, and their rising and falling pitch still tells a Win from a Miss; a Miss stays gentle and low, never a buzzer, because of the no-guilt rule ([concepts.md](concepts.md)).

### 2026-10-08: The Mac's first run matches the iPhone's: quiet hours for the visits, and open at login

The Testing menu's Show the first run plays the flow on a copy. **Why:** the owner asked for the two first runs to match. Quiet hours for everyone keep the "no pings at night" promise the same on both devices, and Settings can turn them off.

### 2026-10-07: On the iPhone, Tomo comes to you with reminders on the reference's cadence; the Lock Screen card is opt-in
The iPhone's first run follows #83 further than the Mac's: after the first word come the rhythm (15 min to daily, **hourly by default**), **quiet hours, 9 PM to 8 AM by default**, "Let words find you" (the only place the iOS notification prompt appears, after its reason), the widget how-to, and the Lock Screen card, which starts only if the learner says yes. Ignored, they stay at the rhythm for two days, then come once a morning, and stop a week after the first. The settings stay on each device; a learner who had the card before keeps it. **Why:** App Review rejected 1.0 partly because nothing said how Tomo comes back, and the owner asked to copy the reference's cadence and defaults. A reminder with a word behind it every time keeps "no guilt" honest, and the back-off spares a learner who stepped away from fifty pings. The card used to start at launch without asking.

### 2026-10-07: One set of screens on the Mac and the iPhone: a window with a sidebar, and tabs

The Mac shows them in the Tomodachi window with a sidebar, in place of the System Settings–style window. The iPhone shows tabs: Play (the play screen, unchanged), Tomo, Words and Settings, with About as the last row of Settings, as in iOS's own Settings. What only one device has comes in through a slot in Settings: open at login on the Mac, reminders on the iPhone ([#88](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/88)). The credits are on About on both, since the CC BY word data needs attribution wherever the app ships. **Why:** the owner wants the two apps in line. A tab bar mirrors the Mac's sidebar and wraps the play screen without changing it. The #83 reference app has no tab bar (a mascot button opens a profile), but its home is a list, while Tomo's play screen is the app. The cost: the tab bar takes about 50 pt from the play screen, which has to fit what's left on small iPhones ([#92](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/92)).

### 2026-10-07: Tomo is never younger than its level, and growth is checked by simulated learners
When a saved Tomo loads, its age is at least its level's age in the pack (saved and logged as a birthday), so each birthday lands on the pack's age boundary even after a pack's ages move. The self-test walks every level of every pack and simulates a perfect and a realistic learner, on a clock that only the check moves, from the first word to the first talking question ([verification.md](verification.md)). **Why:** the owner's "stays at full exp" (#89) was caught by hand; checking growth end to end found English Lv 25 had lost its ten words to a copy of Lv 26 (a `build-levels.py` bug, now fixed), the iPhone cut each evolution short, and a Mac never evolved for a birthday reached on the iPhone.

### 2026-10-07: Esc closes Tomo only when Tomo has the keyboard
Esc is a local key monitor on the island: it works once the learner has clicked Tomo or opened it from the menu (the island takes the keyboard then, and gives it back when it closes), with no permission. During a visit Tomo drops in on its own, Esc stays with the learner's app; × closes the visit, or ignoring it does. **Why:** the old global monitor needed Accessibility, which Tomodachi never asks for, so Esc never worked. A Carbon hot key would have worked anywhere, but it takes Esc from every other app while Tomo is open (vim, dialogs, full-screen video), and Tomo opens by itself while people work.

### 2026-10-07: Testing tools are hidden, and never touch the saved Tomo

The microphone permission is gone while talking questions are choices. **Why:** a learner shouldn't find tools that change Tomo's clock: skipping ahead saved answers dated in the future and synced them to iCloud. The owner tests with Developer ID builds, so it isn't `#if DEBUG`.

### 2026-10-07: Every Tomo is a blob of its own, and evolves at each birthday
Tomo is no longer a chick. Each learner's Tomo is a blob drawn from a seed (`TomoLook`), using blobatar's method and shape primitives (MIT, ported into our code, not its package; [research](research/blobatar.md)): a colour, eye style, mouth, markings and head topper for life, and at every birthday a new silhouette and proportions, a little more size and sometimes an extra, rolled from the seed and the age, with rare shapes as finds. A first version varied only colour and shape, and the owner found the crowd too alike. How different Tomos are is one setting, `TomoLook.variety` (1 = the shipped mix), picked by the owner from `TOMO_RENDER_VARIETY` sheets. Evolution is a first version: shapes re-roll at random each birthday, which doesn't yet read as growing up ([#87](https://github.com/serpcompany/tomodachi-app/issues/87)). The seed is the language pair and the second the Tomo first appeared, which was already saved and synced, so the Mac and iPhone agree with no new iCloud field, and starting over hatches a new Tomo. Widgets, Live Activities and the icons show one fixed mascot (yellow, round), because their frames are fonts made ahead of time. Faces are our own (readable at notch size), drawn in the blob's eye colour. **Why:** the owner found the chick too close to Duolingo, and a Tomo nobody else has, which changes as it grows, is a reason to come back. Accepted: the blob style is blobatar's, so other apps' avatars will look like Tomo's family; we can move our copy away from it over time.
